The model

Websites are never finished. Your budget shouldn’t pretend they are.

The traditional way to buy a website is a big one-time project: a large invoice, a launch day, a handshake — and then silence, right when the real work begins. A subscription matches how websites actually live.

  1. 01

    The web doesn’t stand still

    Content goes stale, browsers change, software needs patching, your business evolves. A site that isn’t cared for quietly rots. Ongoing care isn’t an upsell — it’s the actual job, so it’s built into the price.

  2. 02

    One number, every month

    No estimates, no scope negotiations, no surprise line items. You can plan around it and so can I. A five-figure redesign becomes a predictable monthly cost that flexes with your business instead of ambushing it.

  3. 03

    Trust is built over time

    In month one I learn your business and we launch. By month six I know your customers, your busy season, and which page actually earns you calls. That context compounds — and it’s something no vendor-of-the-week can offer.

  4. 04

    Aligned incentives

    Project shops make their money on change orders and rebuilds. I do well only if your site keeps working for you, month after month — so keeping it fast, healthy, and useful is how I earn my keep.

The difference

Anyone can get a website in an hour now. That’s exactly the problem.

Site builders and AI generators are genuinely impressive tools. But a tool only gives you output. It can’t give you attention.

DIY builders give you tools, not time

With Wix or Squarespace, the subscription buys you software — the work is still yours. You’re the designer, the writer, the SEO person, and the one debugging the layout at 11pm. That’s a second job, not a solution.

AI can generate. It can’t care.

An AI will produce a plausible, averaged version of a website in seconds. It won’t sit with you, ask the second question, notice that your booking page is broken on phones, or call you when something looks off. Judgment and follow-through are the product here — not markup.

You get me. Directly.

No ticket queues, no account managers, no support chatbot. When you email, the person who answers is the person who built your site and will fix it — usually the same day.

Handmade is the fast kind

Every site is written by hand — no page-builder bloat, no fifty plugins. That’s why they load in a blink, work on every device, meet accessibility standards, and are a pleasure for search engines. This page is one; view source and look around.

Questions

Fair questions, honest answers.

Who owns the website?

You do. The domain is registered to you, the content is yours, and if we ever part ways you keep the site and I’ll help you move it. No hostages.

What does “unlimited small updates” actually mean?

Send as many requests as you like; I work through them one at a time, and most are done within a couple of business days. “Small” means things like content changes, new photos, a new section, a tweak to a form. Bigger ideas get planned into upcoming months — that’s the fun part.

What if I need something big, like online booking or a shop?

We roadmap it. Many “big” features can be built incrementally within the subscription over a few months. If something is genuinely beyond that scope, I’ll tell you plainly and quote it separately — no padding, no surprises.

I already have a website. Does this still make sense?

Often, yes. We can start by taking over care of your current site — maintenance, updates, improvements — and rebuild it properly once I understand your business. You don’t have to start from zero to start being looked after.

What happens after the first 12 months?

The plan continues month-to-month at the same price, and you can cancel with 30 days’ notice. Most of the value shows up in the long run — but that should be your conclusion, not a clause in a contract.

What if it isn’t working out mid-contract?

Talk to me. If I can’t make it right, we’ll agree on a fair way to wind down. A relationship held together only by a contract isn’t one I want either.
