The model

Websites are never finished. Your budget shouldn’t pretend they are.

The traditional way to buy a website is a big one-time project: a large invoice, a launch day, a handshake — and then silence, right when the real work begins. A subscription matches how websites actually live.

  1. 01

    The web doesn’t stand still

    Content goes stale, browsers change, software needs patching, your business evolves. A site that isn’t cared for quietly rots. Ongoing care isn’t an upsell — it’s the actual job, so it’s built into the price.

  2. 02

    One number, every month

    No estimates, no scope negotiations, no surprise line items. You can plan around it and so can I. A five-figure redesign becomes a predictable monthly cost that flexes with your business instead of ambushing it.

  3. 03

    Trust is built over time

    In month one I learn your business and we start building something together. By month six I know your customers, your busy season, and which page actually earns you business. That context compounds — and it’s something no vendor-of-the-week can offer.

  4. 04

    Aligned incentives

    Project shops make their money on change orders and rebuilds. I do well only if your site keeps working for you, month after month — so keeping it fast, healthy, and useful is how I earn my keep.

The difference

Anyone can get a website in an hour now. That’s exactly the problem.

Site builders and AI generators are genuinely impressive tools. But a tool only gives you output. It can’t give you attention.

DIY builders give you tools, not time

With Wix or Squarespace, the subscription buys you software — the work is still yours. You’re the designer, the writer, the SEO person, and the one debugging the layout at 11 p.m. That’s a second job, not a solution.

AI can generate. It can’t care.

An AI will produce a plausible, averaged version of a website in seconds. It won’t sit with you, ask the second question, notice your booking page is broken on mobile, or call you when something looks off. Judgment and follow-through are the product here — not markup.

You get me. Directly.

No ticket queues, no account managers, no support chatbot. When you email, the person who answers is the person who built your site and will fix it. I reply within two business days, often sooner.

Crafted, not churned out

Every site is shaped, reviewed, and tested by me — no page-builder bloat, no stack of 50 plugins. That keeps the result fast, portable, and easy to test across modern devices and assistive technology. Search engines appreciate the clean foundation too. Questions

Fair questions, honest answers.

Who owns the website?

You do. Your custom website, content, domain, and third-party accounts are yours. While you subscribe, I may manage the included domain, code repository, and hosting on your behalf, but you’ll always have access to the repository. If we part ways, I’ll transfer the repository and domain, provide the source, assets, and deployment notes, and give reasonable help moving elsewhere. You arrange the replacement hosting — I make sure you have what you need. No hostages.

What does “unlimited small updates” actually mean?

Send as many requests as you like; I work through them one at a time, and most are done within a few business days. “Small” means things like content changes, new photos, a new section, a tweak to a form. Bigger ideas get planned into upcoming months — that’s the fun part.

What kinds of websites aren’t a fit?

I focus on small-business marketing and service websites. Simple booking embeds or payment links may fit. Full online stores and complex web applications usually require a larger budget than this subscription is designed for, but they aren’t off the table. If you have a larger or unusual project in mind, reach out and we can see whether a separate arrangement makes sense.

Who handles domains, email, and paid services?

One standard domain and basic hosting are included. The domain is yours; I handle its registration, DNS, and day-to-day administration during the subscription. Other third-party services stay in your name and use your payment method while I handle setup and integrations through secure access. Any additional provider fees are shown to you first, paid directly, and never marked up.

I already have a website. Does this still make sense?

Often, yes. We can start by taking over care of your current site — maintenance, updates, improvements — and rebuild it properly once I understand your business. You don’t have to start from zero to start being looked after.

What happens after the first 12 months?

The plan continues month-to-month, and you can cancel with 30 days’ notice. Your $200 rate is fixed for the initial year. If pricing changes later, existing clients receive at least 90 days’ notice and new-client pricing will usually change first.

What if it isn’t working out mid-contract?

The first year is a real commitment, not month-to-month; the $2,400 minimum applies. I also understand that businesses can face genuinely difficult circumstances. If that happens, talk to me early. I’ll listen and work in good faith toward a reasonable written solution where I can, but early cancellation isn’t automatic. The agreement protects the up-front work; it isn’t an excuse to treat people unfairly.
