Highest-value opportunities

Make the first step choosing a date.

Picture a parent comparing rentals after bedtime. They have a Saturday, a city, and a group of kids in mind. The site’s job is to answer “what is available?” before another tab does. These are ordered by what interrupts that question.

  1. 01

    High impact · Broken landing state

    Stop the homepage from jumping past the party.

    In a fresh 390-pixel-wide visit, the homepage settled at scroll position 9,528 on a page 14,104 pixels tall. The visible screen was the service-area map and footer rather than the header, rental categories, or booking path. A fresh desktop capture also presented the map as the first visible content. The embedded map or another late-loading layer appears to be taking focus or changing the page position; the exact script responsible needs isolated testing.

    Opportunity: prevent embedded content and promotional layers from moving focus on load, then regression-test the first viewport on common phone widths.

  2. 02

    High impact · Booking

    Bring “Set Event Date” home.

    The Inventory page gets this right: a wide “Set Event Date” control sits above the category grid on desktop and phone. The homepage does not give that task the same priority. It opens with a carousel, reviews, and content sections before the full booking explanation, while the most valuable question—what is available on my date?—lives one navigation step away.

    Opportunity: make date and delivery area the first two inputs on the homepage, then show only inventory available for that event.

  3. 03

    High impact · Mobile fit

    Fit every layer to the phone.

    At a 390-pixel viewport, the homepage measured 415 pixels wide—twenty-five pixels of horizontal overflow. The captured phone view clips the service-area heading and leaves a horizontal scrollbar. On the Inventory page, a large Labor Day coupon popup covers most of the first category card on phone and a substantial corner on desktop, competing directly with the event-date control.

    Opportunity: cap every widget and iframe at the viewport width, reserve popup campaigns for deliberate triggers, and keep the first inventory card fully visible.

  4. 04

    High impact · Accessibility and content structure

    Use customer-facing names in the code, too.

    The homepage has a useful h1—“Explore Our Premium Party Rentals”—but it arrives after a review widget and carousel in the rendered order. Seventeen links had no available text, aria label, or image description, and several images are named “Untitled design.zip,” “Preloader image,” or by export dimensions. Those names describe a production file, not the choice a customer is making.

    Opportunity: put one clear page title before widgets, label each linked image by its rental category, and review unnamed carousel controls separately.

  5. 05

    Medium impact · Trust hierarchy

    Let the reviews support the choice, not become the page.

    A 5.0 rating across 363 public reviews is unusually strong proof. The current widget expands that proof into a long stream of individual cards before the page explains the rentals, cleaning process, and booking flow. The page also carries a video, service-area map, popup service, and tracking integrations around it.

    Opportunity: summarize the rating and count near the first action, feature two recent reviews lower down, and link to the full public listing.

  6. 06

    Medium impact · Maintenance

    Give the copy one careful pass.

    The footer still says Copyright © 2020. Nearby public copy includes phrases such as “So are ready to have a splashin good time,” while other sections repeat title case, all caps, and playful punctuation differently. The tone can stay exuberant without looking unattended.

    Opportunity: update the footer automatically, edit the core booking and safety copy once, and keep a small voice guide for future seasonal campaigns.

One possible direction

Make date, place, and fun the whole first screen.

Direction 01

Booking first, proof close by

The public identity stays playful, while the first action becomes date and delivery area; reviews, categories, and cleaning proof support that choice in order.

A sensible path forward

Repair the landing, then shorten the party.

The first phase is diagnosis and cleanup inside the current site. It should happen before any visual rebuild.

  1. 01
    Isolate the scroll jump

    Test the map, review widget, popup service, and carousel one at a time until the focus or position change is reproducible and removed.

  2. 02
    Fix phone overflow

    Constrain embeds and promotional layers, then verify the homepage and Inventory page at common phone widths.

  3. 03
    Move date selection home

    Reuse the Inventory page’s strongest control as the homepage’s primary action, followed by delivery area and available categories.

  4. 04
    Compress and maintain

    Summarize reviews, defer heavy embeds, rename assets and links, edit the core copy, and update the footer automatically.
