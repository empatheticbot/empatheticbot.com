Highest-value opportunities

Make the phone visit one clean path.

Picture a homeowner who has just found a leak. They want to know whether CHI serves their city, whether the company looks legitimate, and how to get somebody involved. These are ordered by what gets in the way of that decision.

  1. 01

    High impact · Mobile fit

    Rebuild the header for the width people actually have.

    At a 390-pixel viewport, the homepage measured 768 pixels wide. The desktop header remains in place: the logo is cut off, navigation continues beyond the right edge, and the visitor sees only part of the page at a time. The screenshot is not a scaled mock; it is the first visible phone viewport from the live homepage.

    Opportunity: collapse the address and navigation into a compact mobile header, keep call and estimate visible, and regression-test every page at common phone widths.

  2. 02

    High impact · Estimate conversion

    Stop the contact widget from covering the contact form.

    A persistent “Let’s Talk!” panel sits over the lower-right desktop homepage and covers most of the first phone screen. On the contact page it overlaps the estimate form itself. It adds call, file, and message actions beside the page’s existing phone links and form, so a homeowner gets parallel contact systems instead of one obvious next step.

    Opportunity: keep one primary estimate form, one secondary call action, and move photo upload into that same form rather than a separate floating suite.

  3. 03

    High impact · Trust and maintenance

    Let “since 2005” do the math.

    The homepage says CHI has served the area since 2005 and then says “For almost 20 years.” The contact page says “over 18 years.” In September 2026, the same founding date represents twenty-one years. None of the claims is dramatic on its own, but conflicting ages make an otherwise specific history feel copied forward.

    Opportunity: use “since 2005” wherever possible and put dates, hours, totals, and warranty language on a small recurring content checklist.

  4. 04

    High impact · Accessibility and structure

    Name the project imagery and the controls around it.

    Ten of twenty-two rendered homepage images had no useful alternative text. The page also included twenty anchors with an empty destination or a lone #; some belong to theme controls or the contact layer, but they still deserve a component-level review. Star rows are coded as headings, which adds visual symbols to the document outline without describing the reviews they accompany.

    Opportunity: describe meaningful project photos by the work shown, hide decorative images from assistive technology, label every control, and reserve headings for section structure.

  5. 05

    Medium impact · Service-area discovery

    Make every city link lead somewhere useful.

    The Service Areas menu lists a long set of Southeast Michigan cities. A few lead to dedicated roofing pages, while many route back to the homepage. The menu promises local detail but often resets the visitor at the front door without confirming the city, service, or expected response.

    Opportunity: keep only the locations with genuinely useful local content, or use one clear coverage page with a searchable list and a simple “check my address” estimate path.

  6. 06

    Medium impact · Copy quality

    Edit the strongest claims into ordinary language.

    The page has useful proof, but nearby copy says the goal is to provide “professional, friendly, and reliability.” Other sections rely on broad phrases such as “top-notch work” and “unmatched customer service” where the installation totals, license, warranty, and actual project photography could be doing the persuasive work.

    Opportunity: give the core pages one editorial pass and replace generic superiority claims with specific process, warranty, and project evidence approved by CHI.

One possible direction

Put the estimate beside the evidence.

This is an exploratory first-screen concept, not a final design. It uses CHI’s public logo, home photography, phone number, address, license number, services, founding year, and self-reported installation and customer totals. It does not verify those totals or invent final warranty terms.

Direction 01

A responsive estimate front door

The local history and project image remain prominent, while one estimate action, one call action, and a compact proof panel replace overlapping navigation and contact layers.

A sensible path forward

Repair the phone path before redesigning the roofline.

The first phase belongs inside the current site. The redesign becomes useful after the conversion path is stable.

  1. 01
    Contain the mobile width

    Replace the desktop navigation at small widths, constrain every widget, and test every core page at common phone breakpoints.

  2. 02
    Choose one estimate system

    Keep the page form and call action, integrate photo upload there, and remove the overlapping contact panel from conversion pages.

  3. 03
    Correct and structure the content

    Standardize the 2005 history, edit core copy, label imagery and controls, and clean the heading outline.

  4. 04
    Rebuild around proof

    Pair each service with approved project work, a clear process, coverage, warranty language, and one free-estimate path.
