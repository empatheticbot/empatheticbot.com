Highest-value opportunities

Turn a busy homepage into a confident decision.

Picture a homeowner who wants help with a lawn problem but does not yet know the name of the program. They need to identify the need, understand the next step, and know who will follow up. These are ordered by what complicates that path.

  1. 01

    High impact · Quote conversion

    Choose one free-quote destination.

    The rendered homepage contains thirteen links with “free quote” in the label. They lead to /en/get-free-quote, /en/get-free-quote-now, and /get-free-quote. Repetition makes the action loud, but the different destinations make the system harder to maintain and verify.

    Opportunity: route every quote action into one owned flow, keep one primary button per section, and give the visitor a consistent confirmation and response expectation.

  2. 02

    High impact · Mobile readability

    Stop the support widgets from covering the support content.

    On the first 390-pixel phone screen, a rating card and a “Chat Now” graphic overlap the homepage copy. The chat image covers the review card, and together they hide the text immediately after the introductory question.

    Opportunity: remove overlapping launch graphics on small screens, place reviews in the page, and offer chat only after the visitor has had room to understand the service and quote path.

  3. 03

    High impact · Quote usefulness

    Ask what the quote is for.

    The free-quote form asks for name, email, referral source, a general message, and a CAPTCHA. It does not present a phone, property address, service choice, or preferred contact method before “Send,” so the visitor must know what details to invent in the message box.

    Opportunity: start with the service need, then collect the minimum contact and property details required for a useful response; explain what happens and when.

  4. 04

    High impact · First-screen relevance

    Lead with the homeowner’s goal, not a rotating promotion.

    The desktop first screen gives most of its space to a promotional carousel. The active slide advertises 30% off aeration, while the main explanation of what D&B Greener does begins below it. A seasonal discount is useful, but it should not be the only context around the quote button.

    Opportunity: lead with lawn, tree, shrub, and pest outcomes plus one guided quote action; keep current offers as a supporting module with explicit dates and terms.

  5. 05

    Medium impact · Structure and maintenance

    Give the homepage one main heading.

    The homepage uses three h1 elements: “Welcome to D&B Greener of Michigan,” “Affiliations,” and “Why Choose D&B Greener?” Those sections are important, but treating all three as the page title weakens the document outline and makes future editing less predictable.

    Opportunity: keep one descriptive h1, organize the services and proof under h2 headings, and add a small component-level heading checklist.

  6. 06

    Medium impact · Trust maintenance

    Retire the legacy elements visitors can see.

    The rendered page still includes two links to Google+, which closed to consumers years ago. The mobile title bar truncates the company name, and the homepage and footer repeat quote and contact prompts in several styles. Individually these are small; together they make a fifty-four-year company feel less actively maintained.

    Opportunity: remove obsolete social links, simplify repeated utility content, and schedule a quarterly review of promotions, contact details, third-party widgets, and footer links.

One possible direction

Start with the yard, then earn the form.

This is an exploratory first-screen concept, not a final design. Direction 01

A guided quote front door

The 1972 history and real service categories remain visible, while one quote panel begins with the homeowner’s need and keeps the direct phone option nearby.

A sensible path forward

Fix the route before repainting the signposts.

The first phase belongs inside the current site. A broader redesign becomes useful after the quote system and content hierarchy are stable.

  1. 01
    Consolidate the quote routes

    Choose one destination, update every button and menu item, and give the completed inquiry one consistent response message.

  2. 02
    Clear the phone viewport

    Remove or reposition overlapping rating and chat launchers and simplify the stacked utility header.

  3. 03
    Improve the intake

    Ask for service need, property location, contact preference, and message in a short, accessible sequence.

  4. 04
    Rebuild around needs and proof

    Organize services by homeowner problem, keep plans and offers current, and support them with the 1972 story, technicians, affiliations, and approved reviews.
