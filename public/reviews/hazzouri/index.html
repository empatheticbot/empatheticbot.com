Prepared for Hazzouri Accounting & Tax Service

An independent website review · August 2026

A clearer front door for Hazzouri Accounting.

The hard part is already done. There is a real CPA practice here with fifteen years behind it, credentials worth stating plainly, office hours written out for tax season and the rest of the year, and a homepage that sounds like a person rather than a brochure. What the current website asks is that visitors work to find any of it—mostly on a phone, which is where most people looking for a local accountant start.

The short version

The site is stable and the writing is genuinely warm. Its largest constraint is that every page presents itself to the world—and to search engines—as the same page, and that the phone experience is where that costs the most.

What is already working

A sound practice—the website is what is behind.

  • Homepage copy written in a real voice, not stock agency language
  • Office hours spelled out separately for January–April and May–December
  • The phone number on every page, in the header and again on mobile

  • Nothing jumps while the page loads—layout shift and blocking time both measured zero

Highest-value opportunities

Let each page say what it is.

Picture someone in Canton who has just opened a letter from the IRS, or a business owner in March realizing the books are not ready. They search on a phone, they compare two or three local firms, and they decide in under a minute. These are ordered by what they cost that person, not by how easy they are to fix.

  1. 01

    High impact · Search visibility

    Give every page its own name.

    All thirteen reachable pages share one identical title—“Hazzouri Accounting and Tax Service of Canton, Michigan”—and one identical description. In a list of search results, the home health agency page, the individual tax page and the newsletter are indistinguishable from one another. There is no sitemap.xml, the robots.txt file is empty, and no page carries a canonical URL. The automated SEO score is 91, which is high because those checks confirm a title exists; they cannot see that it is the same title thirteen times.

    Opportunity: one specific title and one plain-language description per page, written around the question that page answers—so that “home health agency accountant near Canton” finds the page about home health agencies.

  2. 02

    High impact · Mobile experience

    Take the instructions out of the menu bar.

    On a phone, every page displays the literal text “Click To Open Menu -->” across the navigation bar. It is a comment marker that escaped into the page, and it sits in the first screen a visitor sees, directly beneath the phone number. The menu button next to it works correctly. Below it, the homepage runs about 4,000 pixels on a 390-pixel-wide screen—roughly five full screens of scrolling before the footer.

    Opportunity: remove the stray markup, let the button label itself, and put the two or three things people arrive for inside the first screen instead of below five.

  3. 03

    High impact · Trust and security

    Keep visitors on the secure address.

    Typing https://hazzouriaccounting.com returns a redirect to http://www.hazzouriaccounting.com—the secure address hands the visitor to an unencrypted one, and Lighthouse flags an insecure request on the page itself. For a practice that asks people to send financial details through a contact form, that is worth closing. Alongside it: the blog, linked in the navigation of all thirteen pages, returns a server error; any mistyped .php address returns a server error rather than a proper “page not found”; and the footer still reads Copyright 2012.

    Opportunity: redirect every address to https, add a strict-transport header, repair or retire the blog link, return a real 404 page, and let the footer year update itself.

  4. 04

    High impact · Accessibility

    Move the credentials out of the picture.

    The banner carrying “Khaled Hazzouri, CPA · Certified Public Accountant · Master in Accounting, Wayne State University · Over 15 years of experience · QuickBooks ProAdvisor” is a PNG image, and its alternative text is three dots. Everything that establishes the practice’s authority is invisible to a screen reader and to a search engine. The logo, the portrait, the Business Hours banner and the certification box carry no alternative text at all; the page does not declare its language; the embedded map frame has no title; and the only heading on every page—present twice—is “Contact Us”.

    Opportunity: set the credential block as real text, describe every meaningful image, declare the page language, and let each page’s main heading name that page.

  5. 05

    Medium impact · Forms

    Point the form’s labels at the fields people can see.

    The contact form appears twice in the HTML—one copy for desktop, one for phones—and both copies use the same six element IDs. Because of that, every field label points at the desktop copy, which is hidden on a phone: tapping “Email” on a phone focuses nothing. The script that shows the confirmation message in place is attached to that same hidden copy. Two reCAPTCHA widgets load as a consequence, about 690 KB between them, on a page where one form is ever visible.

    Opportunity: one form that adapts to the screen, with labels and behavior bound to the fields the visitor is actually looking at—and confirmation copy that says what happens next.

  6. 06

    Medium impact · Technical quality

    Ship the page once.

    jQuery loads twice—version 1.12.0, then 1.11.1 over the top of it—and Bootstrap’s stylesheet and script each load twice, across eleven stylesheets and twelve scripts in forty-nine requests to ten different hosts. Four carousel images, about 1.4 MB of PNGs, download on every visit; one of them is ever displayed. Serving those in a modern format alone would save roughly 1.4 MB. The analytics snippet is Google’s ga.js for a Universal Analytics property, and Universal Analytics stopped processing data in July 2023—so there is likely no current traffic data behind any decision about this site.

    Opportunity: one stylesheet and one script, images in a modern format at the size they are displayed, and analytics that is actually reporting, so the next change can be judged rather than guessed at.

Current experience

The issue is easier to see at a glance.

The desktop page holds together. On a phone the same content becomes a long single column, and every decision about what comes first is magnified—including the line of markup across the menu bar.

Audit details

What the measurements say.

Tests were run against the public site on August 18, 2026, using Lighthouse’s mobile profile—a simulated phone on a throttled connection of roughly 1.6 Mbps with a fourfold processor slowdown. Results vary by network, device, cache state, and future content changes.

57/100

Performance

The main image finishes drawing at 14.6 seconds on the simulated phone, and the first text appears at 8.5 seconds. The page weighs about 3 MB, roughly half of it four carousel images. Nothing here is structural—it is mostly weight, and weight is the cheapest thing on this list to fix.

66/100

Accessibility

Missing image descriptions, no declared page language, an untitled map frame, contrast failures and headings out of order. A complete assessment would still require keyboard, zoom, screen-reader and form testing by hand—this score reflects only what a machine can check.

91/100

SEO

The highest score on the page, and the most misleading one. Automated checks confirm that a title and description exist; they do not compare pages to each other. Reading all thirteen by hand shows one title and one description shared between them, no sitemap, and no canonical URLs.

0 shift

Stability

Layout shift and total blocking time both measured zero. Nothing jumps under a reader’s thumb and no script freezes the page. That is a real strength and a preservation goal—a rebuild should be careful not to lose it.

A sensible path forward

Fix the cheap things first.

If Hazzouri Accounting ever takes this on—with anyone—this is roughly the order I’d suggest. The first phase is a few hours of work and would move most of these numbers on its own.

  1. 01
    Stop the bleeding

    Remove the stray menu markup, force https everywhere, fix or remove the blog link, update the footer year, and give the images their descriptions.

  2. 02
    Name every page

    Write one title, one description and one real heading per page, add a sitemap and canonical URLs, and confirm what people actually search for locally.

  3. 03
    Rebuild the phone experience

    One responsive form, task-first ordering, modern images at displayed size, and a single stylesheet and script instead of the current duplicates.

  4. 04
    Keep caring for it

    Working analytics, a yearly content pass before tax season, and a check that hours, forms and contact details still match reality.
