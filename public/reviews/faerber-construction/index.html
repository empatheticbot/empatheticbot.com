What is already working

The site feels like a real local builder.

  • The business is clearly positioned in Canton and Southeast Michigan
  • The logo keeps the licensed-and-insured message and phone number visible
  • Interior and exterior services cover a useful range of homeowner needs
  • Project imagery shows actual work instead of generic stock photography
  • The founder story names David Faerber and nearly twenty years of experience
  • Customer testimonials provide specific names, projects, and outcomes

Highest-value opportunities

Help the homeowner reach the work sooner.

Picture someone planning a deck or bathroom renovation on a phone. They want to recognize the service, see credible work, and decide whether to call or describe the job. These are ordered by what delays that decision.

  1. 01

    High impact · First-screen clarity

    Move the promise above the project photograph.

    On a 390-pixel phone, the header occupies 325 pixels and the first project image fills the next 300. The sentence explaining what Faerber can do does not begin until pixel 635. Desktop visitors also receive a tall logo block, navigation, and image before the value proposition.

    Opportunity: use a compact responsive header and pair a concise project promise with the strongest real project image, one project CTA, and one tap-to-call action.

  2. 02

    High impact · Inquiry conversion

    Make the phone number and project form part of one path.

    The phone number is visible inside the logo but the homepage exposes no telephone link. The only primary action says “Contact Us” and sends the visitor to a separate page before they can describe the project.

    Opportunity: present “Tell us about your project” as the primary action, keep a real tap-to-call link beside it, and bring the first project question onto the homepage.

  3. 03

    High impact · Content structure

    Give every page one clear first-level heading.

    Neither the homepage nor the contact page contains an h1. The homepage’s primary promise is an h3; the contact page title is an h2. The footer copyright is also coded as a heading, so the visible hierarchy and document structure tell different stories.

    Opportunity: use one descriptive h1 per page, follow it with ordered section headings, and remove presentational text from the heading outline.

  4. 04

    High impact · Project proof

    Turn the gallery into case evidence.

    The homepage offers “Before,” “Finished project,” “Exteriors,” and “Interiors,” but the labels repeat and the work has little context. A visitor cannot quickly learn the project type, city, challenge, or result from the image grid.

    Opportunity: give each selected project a short scope, location, before-and-after pair, and homeowner-approved outcome; group the rest by service.

  5. 05

    Medium impact · Accessibility

    Describe the work that matters.

    Four of six rendered homepage images had no useful alternative text. The project photography is the site’s strongest proof, so leaving it unnamed removes context for visitors who use assistive technology and for any interface where the image does not load.

    Opportunity: describe meaningful project photos by the work shown, mark decorative images appropriately, and check every gallery item as it is added.

  6. 06

    Medium impact · Copy and navigation

    Separate the company story from the review story.

    “About Us” and “Why Us?” repeat the same sentence, while the “Read More” link under “Why Us?” opens client reviews. Service copy also includes small editing slips such as “Patios Doors/windows,” which can make maintained content feel less deliberate.

    Opportunity: let About explain who works on the project, let Why Us explain the process and protections, label reviews plainly, and give the service list one editorial pass.

One possible direction

Let the project image and inquiry work together.

This is an exploratory first-screen concept, not a final design. It uses Faerber Construction’s public logo, project photography, phone number, Canton location, service categories, family-owned description, and experience claim. It does not invent final licensing, warranty, availability, price, or project-outcome claims.

Direction 01

A project-led front door

The real deck photograph stays prominent, while a compact header, plain-language promise, project action, phone link, and small proof row make the first visit useful at both widths.

A sensible path forward

Shorten the path before expanding the site.

The first three steps can improve the current site. The concept becomes useful after the content path is agreed.

  1. 01
    Compact the first screen

    Reduce the header, move the service promise upward, and keep the project and call actions visible together.

  2. 02
    Unify the inquiry path

    Connect the phone number, project prompt, and form into one responsive flow with a clear response expectation.

  3. 03
    Structure and edit

    Repair headings, label project imagery, separate About, Why, and Reviews, and proofread the service list.

  4. 04
    Build project stories

    Turn selected before-and-after work into concise case studies that support each priority service.
