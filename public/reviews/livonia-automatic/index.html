Highest-value opportunities

Design for the RFQ, not the theme demo.

Picture an engineer or buyer comparing suppliers with a drawing, quantity, material, and deadline ready. Their fastest useful question is not “can I shop?” It is “can this team make this part, and what do they need from me?”

  1. 01

    High impact · Quote quality

    Put the drawing inside the quote request.

    The public quote form asks for company, contact, service, quantity, desired completion date, and project details. It has no file input. For custom machining, the drawing or CAD file often carries the geometry, tolerances, material notes, and revision that turn a general inquiry into a useful RFQ. The buyer must currently send that critical context through a separate channel.

    Opportunity: add a secure drawing upload with file-type, size, confidentiality, retention, and human follow-up expectations approved by Livonia Automatic.

  2. 02

    High impact · Information structure

    Give each page one real title.

    Neither the homepage nor the request-a-quote page exposed a page-level h1 in the rendered document. On the homepage, major labels such as “Experience That Delivers” and service names are styled in generic containers or paragraphs; on the quote page, “Request a Quote” is also presented without the document’s primary heading role. The visual hierarchy exists, but the semantic hierarchy does not explain it.

    Opportunity: use one specific h1 per page, follow it with ordered capability headings, and keep form group labels below the page title rather than becoming the highest-level structure.

  3. 03

    High impact · Sales focus

    Remove the storefront from the custom-machining path.

    The header includes “Order Online,” the global interface exposes an empty cart, and the footer links to “My Account.” The cart says it is empty and offers “Return to Shop,” while the main site sells technical capability and custom quote work. Unless online shaft and rod ordering is a deliberate, supported line of business, the storefront controls create a second business model in the same navigation.

    Opportunity: either build a complete, clearly separated standard-parts store with documented terms, or remove cart and account scaffolding from the RFQ site.

  4. 04

    High impact · Accessibility

    Describe the shop instead of hiding it.

    Twenty-three of twenty-eight rendered homepage images had no useful alternative text. Some are decorative backgrounds and should remain silent, but capability cards, equipment, production work, and finished parts are meaningful evidence. The public photography is one of the site’s strongest assets; without deliberate names, much of that evidence disappears for screen-reader users.

    Opportunity: classify each image as decorative, linked navigation, process evidence, equipment, or finished work, then write concise alt text only where the image carries meaning.

  5. 05

    Medium impact · Action consistency

    Make every “Request Quote” behave the same way.

    The homepage has a direct link to /request-a-quote/, while other header, button, and footer quote controls use a lone # and open a separate form layer. The rendered homepage also contains hidden quote fields alongside the dedicated route. A buyer should not have to discover whether the same words mean a page, a popup, or no navigation at all.

    Opportunity: choose the dedicated RFQ page as the canonical path, point every quote action there, and preserve draft input if a buyer moves between capabilities and the form.

  6. 06

    Medium impact · Technical credibility

    Tighten the copy to the level of the work.

    The CNC lathe description says inspection ensures “that the we deliver the highest standards,” while the hero ends with “& More...” and several capability sections repeat broad quality language. Technical buyers can be given more concrete help: materials, size ranges, tolerances where approved, equipment context, inspection process, file handling, and the information needed for a quote.

    Opportunity: edit the core pages once, then replace general quality claims with approved capability boundaries, process details, and buyer requirements.

One possible direction

Start with the print, capability, quantity, and date.

This is an exploratory first-screen concept, not a final design. It uses Livonia Automatic’s public logo, shop photography, phone number, address, email, founding year, industries, capabilities, and quote fields. The drawing upload is a recommended concept and would require approved security, privacy, retention, and operations decisions before implementation.

Direction 01

A technical RFQ front door

The real production floor stays central, while the first action becomes sending a drawing and the first proof becomes capability, equipment, industry fit, and clear quote requirements.

A sensible path forward

Clarify the RFQ before adding more marketing.

The strongest improvements organize what the shop already does and make it easier to send complete work.

  1. 01
    Choose one quote path

    Point every quote action to one dedicated route, remove duplicate hidden form states, and define the expected human response.

  2. 02
    Add secure drawing intake

    Approve file types, size, confidentiality, storage, retention, and notification before putting upload into production.

  3. 03
    Repair the content structure

    Add page titles, label meaningful images, edit technical copy, and organize capabilities, equipment, industries, and proof.

  4. 04
    Separate or remove commerce

    Give standard online products their own complete path, or keep the main site focused entirely on technical quote work.
